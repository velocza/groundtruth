    A hackathon prototype for Lafayette, Orinda and Moraga. It shows relative risk on sample data.For safety decisions, follow the National Weather Service and your city.

    Radar sees a slope creep before anyone feels it.

    Sentinel-1 radar measures the ground to the millimeter every 12 days. Bands run downhill where a slope is creeping. Faster bands mean faster ground.

    2 mm per year24 mm per year and up

    Imagery is real (Sentinel-2, 10 m). The motion bands are a simulated sample built on the real terrain, standing in for NASA's OPERA DISP-S1 product until the team downloads the real stack.

              Where each layer comes from

              • ElevationrealUSGS 3DEP through AWS Terrain Tiles, 29 m grid
              • Satellite imageryrealSentinel-2 scenes of 2023-04-19 and 2026-11-04

              • StreetsrealOverture Maps, 1,625 named streets
              • BuildingsrealOverture Maps, 41,494 footprints

              • Ground motionsimulatedStand-in for OPERA DISP-S1 InSAR
              • Risk scoreplaceholderFixed formula until the model is trained

              The model

              The model itself is a gradient-boosted tree ensemble the team writes by hand. It trains on mapped landslides up to 2022 and is then tested on winter 2023, using only what was known each morning.

              A first trial on real labels is in the package: the hand-built model trained on nine of the reported slides and was asked to find the tenth. It scored 5.1 in 10, a coin flip, and plain steepness scored 5.9. Ten street-level reports are too few to learn from, which is why the labels come next.
