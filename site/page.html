            Census estimates for one block group

            Click the map, or pick a block group. Every number is a Census estimate for a whole neighborhood, shown with its margin of error.

            What this is and is not. Census numbers are estimates for a whole neighborhood, not facts about any person or home. The households here are simulated from those estimates: no names, no addresses, and nothing on this page collects anything. The USGS inventory of January 2023 slides is incomplete, so counts from it are a floor. A real ride program would be opt-in only, with vetted volunteers such as a city's CERT team. It would never pair people with unvetted strangers.

                  Limits of this view

                    Real terrain, real rain. The model comes next.

                    Everything on this map except ground motion was pulled from public data for this exact box of hills. The risk score is a hand-set placeholder that marks where the trained model will plug in.

                    Where each layer comes from

                    • ElevationrealUSGS 3DEP through AWS Terrain Tiles, 29 m grid
                    • Satellite imageryrealSentinel-2 scenes of 2023-04-19 and 2026-11-04
                    • RainfallrealNOAA GHCN-Daily, 6 gauges, 2015 to 2026-11-14
                    • StreetsrealOverture Maps, 1,625 named streets
                    • BuildingsrealOverture Maps, 41,494 footprints
                    • Reported slidesreal10 from city and local news reports, street-level positions
                    • Ground motionnot includedOPERA DISP-S1 InSAR needs a NASA Earthdata login. Nothing is simulated in its place
                    • Ground motionsimulatedStand-in for OPERA DISP-S1 InSAR. Shown on the map, not used in the score
                    • Risk scoreplaceholderFixed formula until the model is trained

                    The model

                    The model itself is a gradient-boosted tree ensemble the team writes by hand. It trains on mapped landslides up to 2022 and is then tested on winter 2023, using only what was known each morning.

                    For San Francisco the package also holds 16,658 rain-linked street flooding cells from the city's 311 records. In a first check, the hand-built trees trained on reports before October 2022 ranked the cells that flooded in the November 2026 storm above the rest about 9 times in 10: 8.3 in 10 on where alone and 9.6 in 10 on which days. That is a flooding model, separate from the landslide score on this map.

                    A first trial on real labels is in the package: the hand-built model trained on nine of the reported slides and was asked to find the tenth. It scored 5.1 in 10, a coin flip, and plain steepness scored 5.9. Ten street-level reports are too few to learn from, which is why the labels come next.
